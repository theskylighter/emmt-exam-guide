**Every electron is a tiny magnet, because it spins and because it orbits the nucleus; a material is strongly magnetic only when these tiny magnets fail to cancel and line up together.**

## The Problem: Where Is the Moving Charge?

People knew **lodestone** (magnetite, Fe₃O₄) attracts iron for over 2,000 years, and sailors steered by magnetised needles from about the 11th century. In **1600**, William Gilbert's *De Magnete* showed that the **Earth itself is a giant magnet**. In **1820**, Hans Christian Oersted saw a compass needle twitch beside a wire carrying current: **moving charge makes magnetism**.

That left a puzzle:

- If magnetism needs moving charge, **where is the moving charge inside a lump of iron** with no current in it?
- Why are **iron, nickel and cobalt** strongly magnetic, while **copper and aluminium** are not?

## The Idea in Plain Words

The moving charges are the **electrons inside every atom**. Askeland (Figure 20-1) names two sources:

1. **Electron spin**: the electron spinning about its own axis acts like a tiny bar magnet. Its direction (up or down) is set by the spin quantum number **$m_s$ = +1/2 or -1/2**.
2. **Orbital motion**: the electron circling the nucleus is a tiny current loop, which also makes a magnetic field.

## How It Works

### The unit of atomic magnetism

The magnetic moment of one electron spin is the **Bohr magneton**:

$$\mu_B = \frac{qh}{4\pi m_e} = 9.274 \times 10^{-24}\ \text{A·m}^2$$

### Why most atoms cancel

- In a **filled** orbital, two electrons pair with **opposite spins**, and their moments cancel.
- Filled shells also cancel their orbital moments.
- So only atoms with **unpaired electrons** have a net moment. The important ones are the **transition metals**, whose **3d shell** is only partly filled (see [Quantum Numbers and Spin](01_1_quantum_numbers_spin.html)).
- In a crystal the orbital moments are largely **quenched** (cancelled by interactions with neighbouring atoms), so **spin** does most of the work.

### Why Fe, Co and Ni are special

Having unpaired spins is not enough; Mn and Cr have them too. In **Fe, Co and Ni** (and Gd), a quantum effect called the **exchange interaction** makes the spins of **neighbouring atoms line up parallel**. Millions of aligned atomic moments add together, which is the secret of **ferromagnetism**.

:::example
**Iron** has 4 unpaired 3d electrons, so you might expect 4 Bohr magnetons per atom. Askeland calculates the saturation magnetization and shows that iron actually contributes about **2.1 Bohr magnetons** per atom in the crystal, because the 3d orbital contributions are quenched.
:::

:::key
- Two sources of an electron's moment: **spin** and **orbital motion**.
- Unit: **Bohr magneton**, 9.274 x $10^{-24}$ A.m2.
- Paired electrons cancel, so only **unpaired** electrons (mainly in the 3d shell) give a net moment.
- **Exchange interaction** lines neighbours up in Fe, Co, Ni: ferromagnetism.
:::

:::mistakes
- **"Any atom with unpaired electrons is ferromagnetic."** Unpaired spins are needed, but the **exchange interaction** must also align neighbours parallel (Mn is antiferromagnetic despite 5 unpaired spins).
- **"Copper is non-magnetic because it has no electrons in 3d."** It has a **full** 3d shell (10 electrons), so all spins are paired.
:::

:::exam
- *"Explain the origin of magnetic dipoles"* or *"What is a Bohr magneton?"* (2-4 marks): draw Fig 20-1 and name both sources.
- Often the opening paragraph of a long answer on ferromagnetism.
:::

:::def
The **magnetic moment of an atom** arises from the **spin** of its electrons about their own axes and from their **orbital motion** around the nucleus. Its basic unit is the **Bohr magneton**, $\mu_B = qh/4\pi m_e = 9.274 \times 10^{-24}\ \text{A·m}^2$. Paired electrons cancel, so a net moment arises only from **unpaired electrons**, chiefly in partly filled 3d (and 4f) shells.
:::

## Quick Check

:::q Q1 · Name the two ways an electron produces a magnetic moment.
:::ans
**Spin** about its own axis, and **orbital motion** around the nucleus.
:::
:::

:::q Q2 · Why is a filled electron shell non-magnetic?
:::ans
Electrons in a filled shell pair up with **opposite spins**, so their moments **cancel**, and the orbital moments cancel too.
:::
:::

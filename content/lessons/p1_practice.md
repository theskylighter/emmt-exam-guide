**Practice for Chapters 1 and 2: magnetic materials.** Attempt each question before opening the answer. Long answers give an outline and the lessons to revise.

## A. Definitions and Short Answers

:::q A1 · Define the Bohr magneton and state its value.
:::ans
The **Bohr magneton** is the magnetic moment of a single electron spin, $\mu_B$ = qh/4 pi $m_e$ = **9.274 x $10^{-24}$ A.m2**. See [Where Magnetism Comes From](02_1_magnetic_dipoles.html).
:::
:::

:::q A2 · Define remanence and coercivity.
:::ans
**Remanence ($B_r$ or $M_r$)**: the induction or magnetization that remains when the applied field is reduced to zero after saturation. **Coercivity ($H_c$)**: the reverse field needed to bring the induction back to zero. See [Hysteresis](02_5_hysteresis.html).
:::
:::

:::q A3 · Differentiate antiferromagnetism and ferrimagnetism.
:::ans
| | Antiferromagnetic | Ferrimagnetic |
|---|---|---|
| Neighbouring moments | antiparallel, **equal** | antiparallel, **unequal** |
| Net magnetization | zero | non-zero, large |
| Examples | MnO, NiO, Cr | ferrites (Fe₃O₄, NiZn, MnZn) |
:::
:::

:::q A4 · What are Bloch walls?
:::ans
Narrow boundaries (about 100 nm) between magnetic domains, across which the moment direction changes **gradually and continuously**. See [Domains](02_4_domains.html).
:::
:::

:::q A5 · State the Curie-Weiss law and where it applies.
:::ans
**$\chi = C/(T - T_c)$**, for a ferro- or ferrimagnetic material **above** its Curie temperature, where it behaves as a paramagnet.
:::
:::

## C. Numericals

:::q C1 · A coil of 200 turns and length 0.1 m carries 0.5 A. Find H. If the core has $\mu_r$ = 2000, find B.
:::ans
$H = nI/l = 200 \times 0.5 / 0.1 =$ **1000 A/m**. $B = \mu_r \mu_0 H = 2000 \times 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 1000 =$ **2.51 T** (ignoring saturation; a real core would saturate near 2 T, which is itself a good point to mention).
:::
:::

:::q C2 · A material has $\chi$ = 4999. Find $\mu_r$ and $\mu$.
:::ans
$\mu_r = 1 + \chi =$ **5000**. $\mu = \mu_r \mu_0 = 5000 \times 4\pi \times 10^{-7} =$ **$6.28 \times 10^{-3}$ H/m**.
:::
:::

:::q D1 · Write a note on the ferromagnetic hysteresis B-H loop. (10 marks)
:::ans
**Outline:** (i) definition: B lags H because domain-wall motion is irreversible; (ii) large labelled loop: origin, virgin curve, +$B_s$, $B_r$, -Hc, -Bs, -Br, +$H_c$, arrows; (iii) the four acts with domains: growth, rotation, saturation; remanence; coercivity; loop; (iv) M saturates, B does not (B = $\mu_0$ H + $\mu_0$ M); (v) loop area = energy loss; (vi) loop shape and uses: narrow, wide, square. Revise [Hysteresis](02_5_hysteresis.html) and [Domains](02_4_domains.html).
:::

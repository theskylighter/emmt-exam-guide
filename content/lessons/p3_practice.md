## Section A · Short Answers (10 x 2 marks)

:::q A1 · Define coercivity and retentivity.
:::ans
**Coercivity $H_c$**: reverse field needed to reduce B to zero. **Retentivity (remanence) $B_r$**: induction remaining at H = 0 after saturation.
:::
:::

:::q A2 · State the Curie-Weiss law.
:::ans
$\chi$ = C/(T - $T_c$), valid above the Curie temperature.
:::
:::

:::q A3 · Why is copper diamagnetic?
:::ans
Its 3d shell is full, so all spins pair and cancel; the only response is the induced, opposing (diamagnetic) moment.
:::
:::

:::q A4 · What is $(BH)_{max}$?
:::ans
The largest B × H rectangle that fits in the second quadrant of the B-H loop; a measure of a permanent magnet's power.
:::
:::

:::q A5 · Why are ferrites used at high frequency?
:::ans
They are ceramic insulators with very high resistivity, so eddy-current losses are small.
:::
:::

:::q A6 · What is breakdown in a p-n junction?
:::ans
An abrupt rise in reverse current at a large reverse voltage, when large numbers of carriers are suddenly generated.
:::
:::

:::q A7 · Give the relation between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of a transistor.
:::ans
$\beta = \alpha/(1 - \alpha)$, from $I_E = I_B + I_C$.
:::
:::

:::q A8 · Why does a solar cell have an anti-reflection coating?
:::ans
Bare silicon reflects about a third of incident light; the coating reduces reflection so more photons are absorbed.
:::
:::

:::q A9 · What does the sign of the Hall voltage tell you?
:::ans
The carrier type: negative for electrons (n-type), positive for holes (p-type).
:::
:::

:::q A10 · Which dopants make p-type silicon?
:::ans
Group III acceptors: B, Al, Ga.
:::
:::
